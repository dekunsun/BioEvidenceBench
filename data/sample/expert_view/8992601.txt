  1419 | and F. Pilling observe:—“The Hoodies are generally noticed in largest numbers
  1497 | during thick weather in October and November.” According to Seebohm this
  1570 | species migrates by day, and Gätke says:—“In the autumn, and with favourable
  1647 | weather, the migration commences at about eight o’clock in the morning, with
  1724 | ﬂocks of from ﬁfty to one hundred individuals; the movement soon passes into a
  1803 | stream of ﬂocks, consisting of from a hundred to at least ﬁve hundred examples,
  1883 | and continues in this manner, without gaps of any kind, until two o’clock in the
  1964 | afternoon. We can scarcely, in a case of this kind, assume that we are dealing
  2043 | with a stream or route of migration which just chances to cross Heligoland, for
  2123 | the movement proceeds in equal magnitude from east to west as far as the eye
  2200 | can reach. More than this, on days when powerful migrations of this kind take
  2278 | place, the migration-front or column has been seen from boats eight miles north
  2358 | of the island to stretch farther to the north, as far as the limits of vision extend;
  2444 | while on the south it reached, simultaneously and in equal magnitude, up the
  2521 | Weser, at least as far as Bremerhaven, as was determined from the steamer which
  2601 | regularly plies between this island and the latter place. We thus get a migration
  2683 | column of at least thirty-six geographical miles in breadth.”

   114 | OF the distribution of this species Howard Saunders says:—“To the Faroes
   187 | the Brambling is only an exceptional visitor. On the mainland it breeds
   259 | throughout the sub-Arctic pine and birch forests, from Norway to the
   328 | valley of the Amur: while on migration it occurs in Japan, China, Northern
   403 | India, Asia Minor, and the whole of Europe; but it is only in very severe winters
   485 | that it pushes its wanderings to the African side of the Mediterranean. Immense
   565 | ﬂocks sometimes visit Belgium, Holland, Germany, and Heligoland; but state‑
   641 | ments that this species has nested in the Pyrenees, the Alps, or the Ardennes,
   720 | are as yet unconﬁrmed.” (Manual of British Birds, p. 177).

   780 | To Great Britain the Brambling is chieﬂy a winter migrant, although there
   854 | is reason for believing that a few pairs have occasionally remained to breed with
   936 | us. Although pretty generally distributed throughout our islands in winter, it
  1015 | appears to be rather more numerous in Scotland than in England, whilst in
  1089 | Cornwall, the west of England, and the south of Ireland, it is rarer than else‐
  1169 | where; and in very severe winters it is more abundant, but particularly near
  1246 | beech-woods.

You extract biodiversity occurrence information from one page of a historical natural-history book (19th or early 20th century). The page is given as numbered lines. The text may be a transcription or raw OCR and may contain recognition errors, odd spacing, broken words and stray characters. Never correct, normalise, expand or modernise anything: report what is written on the page.

Extract every MENTION of these four fields. A mention is one occurrence in the text; if the same name appears three times, report it three times. Report mentions in the order they appear. If the page has none, return an empty list.

taxon: a scientific (Latin) name of an organism.
- Include binomials and trinomials ("Accentor modularis"), names of a single word at any rank used as the name of a taxon ("Bulimus", "Turdidae"), and an abbreviated genus followed by an epithet ("A. modularis"). Names in capitals or small capitals count ("BULIMUS CASTUS").
- Exclude the authority or citation after a name: for "Accentor modularis (Linn.)" report only "Accentor modularis".
- Exclude a bare abbreviated genus that only opens a Latin description ("Bul. testa ...").
- Exclude running heads, page headers and plate headings repeated at the top of pages.
- Exclude vernacular (English) names such as "Hedge-Sparrow" or "THE CHASTE BULIMUS".

locality: a place where an organism lives, was found, collected or observed.
- Include place names in habitat, distribution, collection or observation statements. In "Hab. Central America?" the mention is "Central America".
- A place with its narrowing parts is one mention: "Isle of Luzon, Philippine Islands"; "north coast of Norfolk". Different places are separate mentions: "Cornwall and Devon" is two.
- Exclude a leading preposition or relative word: in "near Dover" the mention is "Dover".
- Exclude habitat types without a place name ("woods", "on rocks"), places inside bibliographic citations, publishers, museum or collection names ("Mus. Cuming"), adjectives such as "British", and places in titles or running heads.

date: when an occurrence happened (collected, observed, shot, nested, arrived).
- Include the whole date expression as printed: "3rd May, 1890", "May 1890", "1890", "1880-85".
- Exclude publication years in citations ("Pro. Zool. Soc., 1846"), seasons or months without a year that only describe general behaviour ("in spring", "arrives in April"), ages, durations and page numbers.

collector: a person named as collecting, shooting, finding, observing or sending a specimen.
- Report the name without an honorific: in "shot by Mr. J. Smith" the mention is "J. Smith".
- Exclude naming authorities and cited authors ("Pfeiffer", "Lamarck", "Linn."), and museums, collections or owners ("Mus. Dennison").
